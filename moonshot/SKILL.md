# Moonshot

Imagine the app becomes usable in **0.1 seconds**, serves **a million users**, or retains every
feature in **1,000 lines instead of 10,000**. What would have to be true? What would disappear?
Explore the radical possibility before narrowing it to what feels easy.

Before tuning a steam train's coal feed, consider electric trains, magnetic levitation, helicopters, teleportation that would require invention, or whether the trip is needed at all.

**Standalone:** repeat investigation, build, review, and **full send** for the stated budget;
default **12 hours**. State the start and deadline. **Flavor:** follow the host task's budget, authority, and stopping
point, including read-only limits. Discussing this skill starts no run.

**Full send:** complete delivery through merge, deployment, and live verification where the
user's request and repository rules authorize those actions. Otherwise stop at the last authorized
step. A time budget does not grant extra authority.

This skill works on its own. If installed, use `build`, `review`, `performance-benchmark`, and
`ship` for their matching steps. Otherwise use the procedure below and the repository's tools.

## 1. Imagine, then investigate

Identify the major problem and the user's goal. Question whether the current operation is needed
to achieve that goal. Trace the complete operation, measurements, and previous attempts.
Compare a radical design with the obvious local improvement; follow causes into adjacent systems.

Treat fictional capabilities as hypotheses: name what must be invented and what evidence would
support it.

Choose one relevant thought experiment. Define the usable result and conditions for a speed
target; concurrent operations and data volume for scale; responsibilities removed for simplicity.
A placeholder is not a completed action. Hypothetical users do not justify infrastructure.
Moving complexity elsewhere does not remove it.

Ready: an ambitious outcome, radical alternative, and decisive assumption are explicit.

## 2. Test what must become true

Ask which requests, calculations, copies, or coordination can disappear. If that work became
free, what would still limit the result? Test the hardest assumption cheaply before building
the change or elaborate measurement tooling.

Keep speed, work saved, simplicity, and projected capacity separate. Measure representative
and unfavorable cases. Large ratios on negligible work prove little; projections are not
production proof. Record the baseline, changed result, test conditions, and measurement limits.

Ready: evidence supports a candidate and bounds its claims.

## 3. Build, learn, repeat

Implement the candidate and run the relevant checks. Review the changes for correctness,
regressions, and unnecessary complexity. Fix findings and rerun affected checks. Preserve active
features, saved work, security, accessibility, and invariants. Raise unauthorized product tradeoffs
or paid commitments.

Targets give direction, not acceptance thresholds. Keep worthwhile smaller gains; weigh speed,
resources, maintenance, operating cost, and recovery together. Distinguish tool failures from
design failures; explain revisions, retain results, and rerun relevant proof.

If the moonshot exceeds the budget, test its decisive assumption or build a coherent step toward
it. Do not substitute unrelated easy optimizations. Revisit rejected ideas with new evidence.

Done: finish or safely park before the deadline. For a standalone run, complete authorized full
send; for a flavor, follow the host's stopping point. Report gains, costs, limits, and unproved
work. Close owned resources.
